# Gate 1 baseline

Gate 1 of [`../docs/plan.md`](../docs/plan.md): the ablation ladder that
settles what each term of the decomposition is worth, on splits fixed
before anything was fitted. Generated by `scripts/run_baselines.py` from
the data under `data/challenge/` on 2026-09-11; every figure below is
computed there, and the generator refuses to write a literal stand
identifier from either source.

## What is fitted, and on what

2,085,047 training departures: the 2025 movement files in full.
The feature frame is `taxiout.features`: the airport, stand and runway
identifiers; the take-off clock; the distance-matrix cell and four shape
ratios; the join-quality flags; and `LOBT_flt - IOBT_flt`. Every column
is registered in `FEATURE_SOURCES` against the raw columns it reads, and
`tests/test_features.py` asserts that no source is in
`taxiout.challenge.FORBIDDEN_FEATURE_INPUTS` and that poisoning those
columns does not move the frame.

Boosted rungs use lightgbm with the fixed parameters in
`taxiout.models.BOOSTER_PARAMS` -- `n_estimators=1000`, `learning_rate=0.03`, `num_leaves=127`, `min_child_samples=50`. There is no early stopping, because stopping on the set being
reported is tuning on it, and nothing here was chosen by a leaderboard
score.

## The unimpeded term

Two estimates of the same quantity. The PRU reference is the organisers'
own: the 10th percentile of observed taxi-out per
(airport, stand, runway), over pairs seen at least 30 times in
the training side of the split. The geometric version is an intercept per
airport and wake class plus `taxi_m` over a per-airport speed, modulated
by route shape, and so is defined for every pair the matrix can route,
seen or not.

On the December split's training rows the reference answers at pair level
for 97.7% of departures, falls back to (airport,
runway) for 2.3% and to the airport for
0.0%. The fallbacks are the gap the geometry
exists to fill.

### The fit

One least squares of the pair's reference on an intercept per (airport,
wake class), `taxi_m` per airport, and `taxi_m` times each standardised
route descriptor, pooled -- over the unflagged lower tail, the 185,084
rows at or below their own pair's reference, restricted to pairs that have
one and to rows whose geometry is a real matrix cell. A class with fewer
than 30 such rows at an airport is pooled into `other`.

The intercept is the pushback, start-up and line-up that no distance
explains, and it is the reason the Gate 1 speeds -- fitted through the
origin -- came out at 3 to 6 m/s, below any aircraft's taxi speed: they
were carrying it. `reports/geometry_gaps.md` measured it, and measured
that it differs by wake class at the same stand and runway. Seconds, per

## The ablation ladder

Each rung adds one term of the decomposition. The step that matters is
`identifiers + time` to `identifiers + time + geometry` --
geometry added to a model that already has the identifiers -- and on a
forward split it is expected to be small, because
a saturated identifier model has seen every pair it is asked about. The
held-out-pair split below is where the difference is supposed to appear.

Two rungs need reading with care. The PRU reference and the geometric
term both estimate *unimpeded* taxi-out and are being scored against
actual taxi-out, so they are biased low by the entire excess term and
will sit above the airport mean for a reason that has nothing to do with
how good either of them is. The `+ airport offset` column adds back a
per-airport constant fitted on the same training rows -- the excess held
as a constant, which is exactly what Gate 2 exists to replace with a
model -- and is the column to compare them on. The boosted rungs predict
taxi-out directly and need no offset, so it reads n/a for them.

The plan-lag rung reads three exposed planning values -- the flight
plan's first and last off-block estimates and the schedule -- as take-off
minus a planning value fixed before departure, so taxi plus the pushback
delay against the plan; the subject's own, and the mean over the other
departures on its runway within fifteen minutes of its take-off, the
subject removed by identity. It sits after the aircraft and before the
congestion blocks, so every congestion rung above it is read net of the
plan lags. The same congestion rungs without the plan lags are kept at
the foot of the ladder, each read against the one before it in that
chain, so the reader sees what each block is worth both ways.

The last rung of the ladder is not the model, and the ladder is not one
chain. Above the weather rung sit three encoding arms, which are
alternatives to one another rather than steps; above the arm those chose,
the four Gate 2c blocks, as a chain of their own; and above everything,
runway saturation, which was built as the cheap queueing proxy, measured
against the ceiling and found to be worth nothing, and which is read
against the weather rung it was added to rather than against whatever the
submission has since moved to. The encoding arms and the Gate 2c blocks
each have a section below with the rule that admitted or rejected them;
the saturation rung's measurement against the ceiling is in
[`congestion.md`](congestion.md). The submission is
fitted from `+ plan differences`, and the per-airport, strata and
split-share sections read that rung.

## Held-out pairs

3 folds. Whole (airport, stand, runway) pairs are dealt round-robin
within each airport, so every fold holds out a random third of every
airport's pairs -- a volume-representative third, not the rare ones; the
rare-pair question is the strata table's below. Each fold fits one model
and scores it twice: on the held-out pairs, and on a
5% sample of rows whose pairs stayed in training and which were
themselves excluded from the fit. Same model, same period, same airports;
the only difference is whether the pair was seen.

**The held-out unit is the pair, not the stand.** Every stand and every
runway is still seen in other pairs, so the identifier model is never
asked about a stand it has not met -- only about a combination. This
bounds what the geometry buys on a new pair; it does not isolate what it
would buy on a new stand, which no split of this data can measure.

**The claim is a difference in differences.** What a block buys on seen
pairs is one number; what it buys on held-out pairs is another; the
claim is that the second exceeds the first. Read that way, whatever
composition difference exists between the two row sets cancels, because
it enters both rungs alike -- and it is small regardless: the airport
mean, which knows nothing about a pair, moves by +12.5 s [-2.9, +29.8] between the two.

**Intervals are a cluster bootstrap over pairs**, 1,000 draws, resampling pairs with replacement from the stored
residuals and recomputing every RMSE from them -- no model is refitted.
Rows within a pair share a stand, a runway and a route, so the pair is
the unit. The interval is the 95% percentile interval of the fold-mean.

All figures below are over the rows the record-quality term leaves to
the physical model: one defaulted block time in a 5% sample moves a
raw RMSE more than the whole geometry block does. The raw, every-row
gaps are at the end, for the record.

## The shrunk identifier encoding

An empirical-Bayes encoding of the (airport, stand, runway) pair, shrunk
toward the *geometry* rather than toward a group mean
(`taxiout.encoding`):

```
encoded(pair) = (n * ybar_oof + k * g_hat) / (n + k)
```

`ybar_oof` is the out-of-fold mean of taxi-out over that pair's unflagged
training rows, `g_hat` is the geometric unimpeded prediction for that pair
plus the per-airport offset that puts it on the same scale as the target,
and `k` is estimated rather than chosen. The same construction at stand
level is the second feature.

**Why the geometry is the prior.** Shrinking toward the airport mean is the
standard remedy and the airport mean knows nothing about this stand: it is
the same number for a stand at the far corner of the field and one beside
the runway entry. The geometry is the only prior here that knows which is
which. The strata table above is what motivates it -- the top rung has no
deficit on pairs it has never seen, where it already falls back gracefully;
the deficit is on pairs seen once to ten times, where the identifier exists
but its mean is a handful of draws.

*How much* of the ranking set is thin is a property of the data and of the
geometry rather than of this block, and is measured in
[`geometry_gaps.md`](geometry_gaps.md) rather than repeated here. What this
section needs from it is the shape: the thin pairs are a small share of the
ranking rows, they are concentrated at a few airports and in the summer
month, and the never-seen ones are the *smallest* of the thin strata. The
ceiling below is computed from that shape and from the strata table above.

and the 0.95 s between those two numbers is what the buckets held at zero would take back -- more than the floored figure itself, so the ceiling is positive only because of that convention.

Read against that: the buckets below `>100` are 8.8% of the rows and carry every second of the ceiling, so a block aimed at them is aiming at 0.30 s pooled and no more. An arm that moves the pooled figure by much more than that is not doing what it says it is doing.

### What `k` came out at

`k = sigma2_within / sigma2_between`, **per airport**: geometry that fits
one airport well and another badly must be shrunk toward differently, and
a single `k` across ten airports would be one number answering ten
questions. Both variances are moments of the *residual* `y - g_hat` and
are shown as standard deviations, in seconds, because a variance in
seconds squared is unreadable.

The column that matters is the correction. The observed spread of group
residuals is the true spread *plus* the sampling noise in each group's own
mean; `sigma2_between` is the first minus the second, and the last column
is how much `k` moves because of it. Without the subtraction every `k`
here would be smaller and every thin pair would be shrunk less than the
evidence warrants. An airport whose corrected between-variance falls to
the guard's floor is marked instead of given a percentage: there the
number would be the floor's and not the correction's.

`k over the route range` is filled only where the length dependence below
was found material, and then it is the `k` the feature actually uses --
at the 10th and 90th percentile of that airport's route length. Where it
is blank the single `k` is the one in force.

Groups are those with at least 30 rows in the sample the estimate is
made from -- below that a group mean is a handful of draws rather than a
mean, and putting it in the between-variance estimates the noise instead.
Inside an inner fold that sample is four fifths of the fit rows, so the
threshold there falls on a group's out-of-fold count and not on its total.

**Two limits of this estimate, stated rather than left to be found.** The
between-variance is measured on groups with at least 30 rows and then
applied to groups with one -- which are the groups this whole block exists
for. That extrapolation is the price of estimating a variance at all: a
thin group's mean is mostly noise, so it cannot tell you how far group
means spread. And the correction subtracts a *pooled* within-variance
rather than each group's own, because a three-row group's own variance is
itself two degrees of freedom of noise. Both choices are conservative in
the same direction -- they make the between-variance look larger and `k`
smaller, so the encoding shrinks less than a perfect estimate would.

**Is the within-group variance length-dependent?** A longer route has
more room to vary, so the constant within-variance was checked against a
length-dependent one, by the rule fixed in `taxiout.encoding` before it
was run: material if the fitted within-variance at the 90th percentile of
route length is at least
1.5 times its value at the 10th, row-weighted across
airports. The ratio is read off the fit *before* the variance floor is
applied, and an airport whose fitted variance is not positive at both
ends abstains -- flooring first would pin the short end at the guard's
value whenever the true dependence is faster than linear, and a check
that can only ever overstate is not a check. The answer is read only if
the airports that vote carry at least half the rows.

Voting airports carry 93.5% of the rows, which is enough. Measured ratio **1.29**, so it is **not material**, and the constant within-variance stands. The length-dependent fit was made, compared and discarded; it is not that it was never checked.

**The prior's offset.** `g_hat` on its own estimates *unimpeded* taxi-out
and `ybar` is a mean of actual taxi-out, so the two differ by the whole
excess term. Without an offset the prior would drag every thin pair low by
minutes, which is not shrinkage but bias. The offset is one number per
airport fitted on the same rows -- 194 to 411 s,
median 217 s -- and is the same quantity the ladder's
geometric rungs carry in their `+ airport offset` column.

**The ranking-mix column** is the December strata pooled at the bucket weights the ranking set carries rather than December's own, by the arithmetic in *Error by pair frequency*. It is a reweighting of measured numbers and not a score on the ranking set, whose targets are unseen. It is here because it is the quantity the competition scores and because the December column understates a thin-pair block: the ranking set holds roughly twice December's share of rows in the two thinnest buckets.

**What the last column can and cannot see.** On a held-out pair the pair encoding is `n = 0` and is therefore *identically the prior* -- that is the leakage guard working, and it is also a blind spot. The difference in differences can only test the stand-level encoding, which survives because the stand is still seen through its other pairs, and the prior itself. It says nothing about the pair encoding, whose whole purpose is a pair seen a few times rather than not at all. That evidence is the frequency strata, and it is in the table below. This is not a caveat on the column but the reason the rule below voids it: a gate this blind to the feature under test cannot reject it.

**Intervals are at 98.3%, not 95%.** Three arms are tested and the
largest is kept, so a 95% interval per arm is a 14% chance that one of them
clears zero on noise. The level printed is Bonferroni over the three, which
is the crude correction and is used because the three arms are the same
block spent three ways and their errors are not independent. Note also what
the interval is: a cluster bootstrap over pairs of the *stored residuals*,
with no model refitted, so it carries the sampling variation of the rows and
not the variation of the fit, and is a lower bound on the uncertainty.

**The original rule, and why its first half was voided rather than failed.** The rule fixed before the numbers was: an arm displaces the control only if its difference in differences is positive with an interval clear of zero, *and* it is no worse than the control on either forward split. The first half **cannot credit the component the block exists for.** A held-out pair encodes to `n = 0` and is identically the prior, so on every held-out row that column is a deterministic function of the geometry carrying none of the pair's own mean. The thing the block was built to supply is absent from the split exactly where the split is measuring, and no arrangement of it could earn credit there.

**The scope of that, stated precisely,** because overstating it would be self-serving. The gate is applied to a whole *arm*, and an arm is more than its pair column: two of the three also remove the `stand` categorical, and the stand-level encoding survives a held-out pair through the stand's other pairs. Those parts the fold measures properly, and it measures them as negative. The difference in differences is therefore **not** an instrument incapable of returning a positive number for an arm. It is an instrument blind to the pair encoding -- and a gate that cannot see the thing under test is not a test of it.

So that condition is **voided as a test of the pair encoding**. Voided is not passed: the arms take no credit from it, and the column is still computed and printed above, reported as the narrower thing it is -- a valid and decisively negative reading of the stand-level treatment and of the prior.

**The rule actually used.** On the two instruments that can see a pair encoding: the forward splits and the frequency strata.

1. no worse than the control on **both** forward splits' ex-flagged RMSE -- carried over from the original rule, unchanged;
2. no worse than the control **pooled at the ranking set's bucket mix**, which is the quantity the competition scores and which December's own mix understates for a thin-pair block;
3. among the arms passing 1 and 2, the one that **keeps the runway categorical**. This is a mechanism, not a preference. [`geometry_gaps.md`](geometry_gaps.md) measures what the designator carries that the routed distance cannot compute: an intersection departure enters the runway at a taxiway junction rather than at the threshold, and the signature of that is up to 96 s per designator and constant within one -- invisible to a matrix that routes to the threshold, absorbed by any model that knows the designator, and *not* recoverable by an encoding shrunk toward that same distance. So replacing the stand is admissible and replacing the runway with it is not, for a reason measured before these arms were run and independent of their numbers.

The never-seen (`0`) bucket **corroborates and does not decide.** It points the same way -- the stand-replacing arm degrades far less there -- but it is a few hundred rows, and a decision resting on it would be a post-hoc rule resting on a thin stratum. It is reported below as agreement, not as the reason.

**This replacement was written after the numbers were seen.** That is a real weakening of the original design and is said here rather than left for a reader to notice. Three things limit it. Condition 1 is the original rule's own second half, unchanged, and it is the half that does the rejecting. Condition 3 only chooses *between* arms that already pass, and can never admit one that would otherwise fail, so it has no power to turn a negative result positive. And condition 3 is a mechanism measured in another report before these arms existed, rather than a threshold read off their results -- which is the difference between a rule and a rationalisation.

**Which condition actually binds, on this evidence.** `+ shrunk encoding` falls at condition 1, at +2.8 s on July. Condition 2 rejects nothing here: every arm that clears the forward splits also clears the ranking-mix figure, because the reweighting moves each arm and the control in the same direction. It is stated as a condition because it is the quantity being optimised for and an arm could have cleared December while losing on the mix that is scored -- not because it did any work on this data. Saying so is the point: a rule whose conditions are not all load-bearing should admit which ones were.

On this evidence `+ shrunk encoding, replacing the stand` is the arm that keeps the runway categorical among the 2 that clear both surviving conditions, and the designator is not the model's to give up: `geometry_gaps.md` measures an intersection signature of up to 96 s per designator, constant within a designator, that the routed distance cannot compute because it routes to the threshold. An encoding shrunk toward that distance cannot carry it; the categorical can. The never-seen bucket agrees rather than decides: +6.7 s against +15.1 s. The pooled figure agrees with it -- 232.9 s against 233.2 s at the ranking mix, so the arm that keeps the runway is also the best of them pooled and nothing is being traded away for the mechanism.

**Chosen: `+ shrunk encoding, replacing the stand`.**

**And shipped, through the rung above it.** `taxiout.models.SUBMISSION_KEY` is `plan-diffs`, which carries this arm's whole feature block and adds the Gate 2c blocks on top of it, so the encoding is in the submitted model and everything said above about how it is built at inference still holds. What admits the blocks above it is *The Gate 2c blocks* below.

### What the arms actually did

2 of the 3 arms improve *both* forward splits, and the arm the rule chooses is one of them. What it bought is decomposed here rather than asserted, because the rung's name says where the block was aimed and the decomposition says where it landed, and those are not the same place.
Take the arm the rule chose, `+ shrunk encoding, replacing the stand`, and split what it bought. It is not the strongest on the `1-10` bucket -- `+ shrunk encoding, replacing stand and runway` is, by 1.4 s -- and the arm that ships is the one to account for.

On pairs the model had already seen it is worth **+4.1 s**. On pairs
held out of training it is worth **-1.3 s**.
That is what it is: the encoding is **memorisation, smoothed**. Smoothing is why it survives a forward split, where almost every pair has been seen; the held-out fold is why it cannot be called a geometry feature. It is reported under that name rather than under the rung's, which was written for what the block was aimed at.

The strata locate it. Of the +2.49 s it moves the pooled
December figure, **+2.08 s** comes from the `>100` bucket -- pairs seen more than a hundred times, 91.2% of the rows, which
by the ceiling above has no headroom at all -- and **+0.40 s** from every
thinner bucket together. The block was aimed at the thin buckets. It landed on the thick one.

On the `1-10` bucket itself it takes +9.3 s of the 27.8 s of headroom that bucket has.
The bucket is 0.64% of the rows, so whatever it does there is
worth +0.09 s pooled and no more.

On the `0` bucket -- pairs never seen -- every arm is *worse* than the control. That is the distribution shift the held-out folds measure, seen from the other side: a column carrying a real pair mean in training and the bare prior at inference is a column whose meaning changed between fit and use, and the booster leaned on it in training regardless.

### What the chosen arm is, in its own terms

The same decomposition at the ranking set's bucket mix, which is the mix the competition scores and the one the rule's second condition uses. In the sum-of-squares domain, where the buckets are additive:

| bucket | share of the ranking-weighted gain |
| --- | ---: |
| `0` | -1.4% |
| `1-10` | +7.3% |
| `11-100` | +13.2% |
| `>100` | +80.9% |

**81% of the gain comes from the `>100` bucket** -- pairs seen more than a hundred times, which by the ceiling above has no headroom at all -- against 20% won at `1-10` and `11-100` together, and 1.4% given back at `0`.

So the honest description of this block is not the one its rung name implies. It is **smoothed memorisation that buys real score on well-observed pairs**. It was built to help where the pair is thin; it does a little of that, and most of what it earns it earns somewhere else. The score is real and is why the rule chooses it. The mechanism is not the one it was aimed at, and the report says so rather than letting the rung's name imply otherwise.

**It is also a geometry-bearing feature**, because the prior it shrinks toward is `g_hat`. That has a consequence for any ablation of the geometry: an ablation that removed the geometry block and left the encoding in would not have removed the geometry, and the contrast it produced would shrink as an artefact of its own definition rather than as a finding. Two things hold here. The claim contrast above is `identifiers + time` to `identifiers + time + geometry`, and **neither rung carries the encoding** -- the arms sit at the top of the ladder and the claim is measured near its foot -- so that figure is untouched by this decision, by construction and not by luck. And `taxiout.models.Rung.uses_geometry` now counts `ENCODING_FEATURES` as geometry-bearing, so a rung carrying the encoding cannot be classified as geometry-free if the claim is ever re-anchored higher up the ladder. The encoding is removed by the ablation; it was not replaced with an airport-mean-shrunk version, which would have needed a refit and would have measured a different feature.

### What was guarded, and what was not

Four leakage rules, each with a test in `tests/test_encoding.py`, because
any one of them missed silently makes the number *better* rather than
raising an error:

1. The encoding is recomputed **inside every fold**, from that fold's own
   training rows. A pair held out of a fold sees `n = 0` and encodes to pure
   `g_hat`; the test asserts exactly that. Computed once over all training
   rows and reused, every held-out pair would arrive carrying its own mean
   and the contrast above would be a measurement of the bug.
2. `ybar` is out of fold **within** training too, by 5-fold over the fit
   rows, so a row's own target is never in its own feature. The test poisons
   one row by an hour and asserts its own encoding does not move. `k` is
   estimated out of fold for the same reason -- with a single `k` over all
   the fit rows that test failed by twelve seconds, which is how the
   out-of-fold `k` came to exist.
3. `ybar` reads **unflagged rows only**. One defaulted block time at 50,000 s
   inside a three-row pair would otherwise be that pair's encoding. The test
   plants exactly that row and asserts nothing moves.
4. `g_hat` is fitted on rows that **also form `ybar`**. This one is not
   guarded, because it cannot be: the prior is a fit over hundreds of
   thousands of rows and a thin pair contributes a handful of them, so the
   overlap is negligible for exactly the pairs this block exists for -- but
   it is an overlap, and it is stated here rather than left for a reviewer
   to find.

**The ranking set carries no fold.** At inference the encoding is computed
once over the whole 2025 training year and read by the 2026 ranking rows.
That is 2025 predicting 2026 across a boundary no ranking row is on either
side of: there is nothing to be out of fold of, and the guards above are
about the ladder's own splits and not about the submission. The registry
says the same: the encoding's sources carry a `TRAIN.` prefix naming the
target of the rows the model is fitted on, which is a source class
`FEATURE_SOURCES` did not carry before this block and which is deliberately
not written as `DEP.TAXITIME_SEC_mvt` -- the ranking set blanks that field
for every departure, and a feature claiming to read it would be claiming
something false.

### The same block without the subject's take-off

A diagnostic beside the chain, on no rung of it and in no submission. **The leakage audit, applied.** `+ stand occupancy` takes +15.7 s on July, the three against an estimated +10.3 s, which is more than the 5 s the trigger allows. On July, the three it fires by 0.37 s, inside the 1.28 s noise floor on that leg: the verdict is within what changing the seed alone produces, a near miss on the other side of the line. Read literally, the rule above says a block that trips the audit is not a success. The chain's rule does not read the audit, and the block stays in the chain and in the submission: an overshoot inside the seed floor cannot tell leakage from noise. That reading was adopted after the number was seen, and the report says so in those words. There are two readings of an overshoot and the difference between them matters, so both are stated.

**The two instruments disagree -- the refit takes 1.52 times what the headroom estimated -- and that is the finding.** The headroom was estimated by fitting a small booster on the *stored residuals* of a frozen model, cross-validated within the validation month itself -- 165,677 rows -- while the figure it is being compared against comes from refitting the whole model on the training year, 1,919,370 rows. Those are not two measurements of one quantity. A residual fit can only add a correction on top of a prediction it cannot change; a refit restructures every split in the model around the new column and can use it to condition the blocks already there. So a residual fit is expected to come in *under* a refit. What is not established is the size of that gap: nothing here predicted the ratio above, no theorem bounds it, and reading the direction as confirmation would be reading a prediction out of a result. The honest statement is that the trigger fired and that, on this evidence, the estimate is the weaker of the two instruments.

**The other reading is leakage**, and it is the one the trigger exists for: a block cannot buy more than the information it carries, so an overshoot is consistent with its carrying something it should not. The registry names every raw column each of these features reads and `tests/test_features.py` holds departures' sources to `FORBIDDEN_FEATURE_INPUTS` and arrivals' to `AVAILABLE_FOR_ARRIVALS`. The poison tests assert the code and not the registry: poisoning every departure's hidden fields leaves the frame unmoved, and poisoning the arrivals' on-block times moves the `occ_` columns and the arrival counts and nothing else. Those two bound what the block *can* read. Where what it read actually landed is bounded by the decomposition above -- *Where the stand-occupancy gain lands* -- which is a measurement rather than the agreement of two estimates, the thing that failed.

## Seed averaging

An estimator choice, reported beside the booster's parameters rather than as
a rung of the ladder, because it changes how the model is fitted and not what
it reads. `+ plan differences` is refitted under 5 consecutive random
seeds on each forward split -- the same rows, the same encoding, the same
parameters, only the seed moving -- and the predictions averaged. It is run
on **one rung** rather than on every rung, after the feature decisions were
taken, so that the ladder is not refitted for it.

## The recorded-taxi tail, out of the fit

The record-quality term removes rows by *cell* -- an airport crossed with whether a Network Manager record exists and how long the take-off lag is -- and a cell is only covered where enough of its rows are defaulted. A row whose cell is uncovered stays in the fit with whatever taxi time it records, and some of those records are day-shifts: the largest in the training year is over thirty-six hours. This is what taking them out of the **fit** is worth. The target is not clipped and the validation rows are untouched, so every figure here is over the same rows as the ladder's.

**Why an exclusion and not a wider flag.** `taxiout.tails.flag` and `predict` are restricted by construction to fields the ranking set exposes, because they have to run there unchanged. Recorded taxi time is the target and is hidden for departures in the ranking set, so a rule that reads it can only ever apply to the training side. That is what this is, and it is why widening the flag was not available.

**Both thresholds were fixed before the run and both are reported.** 4 h and 2 h, in `TAIL_EXCLUSION_H`. Four hours is the mechanism -- no aircraft taxis for four hours -- and two hours is the wider reading, kept because the boundary between an impossible row and a very bad day is not obvious. Reporting only the one that came out better was not available as a move, and neither threshold was chosen by the figure it produced.

**The rule, fixed before the numbers.** An arm is adopted only if it is no worse than the un-excluded fit on **both** forward splits within 0.1 s, **and** no airport regresses by an amount whose own day-clustered interval clears zero. The second half is there because the reason for looking at the tail at all is that a pooled figure hid what one airport was doing.

The reference row is the same rung fitted with nothing excluded. It is refitted here rather than read off the ladder, because the ladder now carries the adopted exclusion and reading an arm against it would be reading it against itself.

## The noise floor

A fit that repeats to the bit makes a run repeatable, and says nothing
about whether a small difference between two fits means anything. A fit's
figure still moves with its random seed -- through the rows and the columns
each tree samples, and the sample of rows its histogram bins are cut from --
and the seed-averaging section above refits `+ plan differences` under 5 seeds on each forward split. The spread of those single-seed figures is how far a figure moves when nothing but the seed does.

| leg | range of the 5 single-seed ex-flagged RMSEs (s) | standard deviation (s) | floor (s) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| December, all ten | 217.75 to 218.08 | 0.13 | 0.19 |
| July, all ten | 325.58 to 326.25 | 0.28 | 0.39 |
| July, the three | 235.94 to 238.09 | 0.91 | 1.28 |

The floor is the standard deviation times the square root of two, fixed before the first run that printed it. A comparison between two boosters is the difference of two single-seed fits, and the square root of two is the spread of such a difference when the two fits' seed noise is independent. They share one seed value, so that is an approximation; where the shared seed makes them move together the true spread is smaller, and the floor errs towards calling a difference noise. A comparison smaller than the floor is within what changing the seed alone produces. Four limits, stated rather than hidden. The spread is measured on one rung and assumed for the others. With 5 seeds the standard deviation is itself uncertain by about 35%. The PRU reference and the geometric rungs draw no seed, so where one of them is on a side the floor overstates the seed's share; they are listed by the same rule. And the held-out-pair folds have no seed refits, so their contrasts are not covered here; their own intervals are in their section.

The comparisons the floor was fixed to cover are each rung against the rung it is read against and each tail-exclusion arm against the fit with nothing out. The report draws others -- one encoding arm against another, the k-sensitivity refits, the seed average against one seed -- that are not checked here. Of those covered, 13 of 31 have a change in ex-flagged RMSE below the floor on at least one leg, and are listed. A change in bold is below the floor on its leg; the test is on the unrounded figures, so a change printed equal to its floor can sit on either side of it. Negative is better.

## What no split here can answer

Every split above is a split *within* the ten airports the training year
contains. The held-out-pair folds take away a pair and keep the airport,
which bounds what the geometry buys on a stand the model has not met and
says nothing about a *field* it has not met. That question --- what the
model does at the eleventh airport --- is the operational one, the
challenge's test set cannot pose it, and no identifier-based entry can
answer it at all. It has its own report:
[`transfer.md`](transfer.md), ten fits that train on nine airports and
predict the tenth.

## What is not here yet

The queueing layer (Gate 3) is absent. Every other layer of the
decomposition now has a rung above, so the forward splits are no longer
a model of the unimpeded term alone; they should still be read as a
floor rather than as an estimate of what the entry can do, because the
queueing layer is where the remaining excess sits.
